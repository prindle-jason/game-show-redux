# Next milestone: Wheel of Fortune round, end to end

**Status: Complete.** `typecheck`/`lint`/`test`/`build` are green across the monorepo; the reduce state machine (turn rotation, spin/buy-vowel/guess economy, host-judged solve, skip/end-round overrides) and the board UI (host puzzle/judge/override panel, contestant spin/buy-vowel/solve controls) are implemented and unit-tested. The three-browser-tabs manual playthrough has been confirmed, including a follow-up round that fixed a repeat-`end-round` scoring bug and three UX gaps found during that playtest (see "Implementation deltas" below).

## Context

Jeopardy and Final Jeopardy proved the round-type plugin pattern from two different angles: a buzz race with host arbitration, and a hidden-then-revealed wager flow. Wheel of Fortune is the third and most stateful of the three — a turn-based loop with a random element (the spin), player-driven economy (buying vowels), and a solve attempt that, per this milestone's requirements discussion, needs host judgment rather than automatic string matching. It's also the first round type that needs genuine randomness in a codebase built around pure, unit-testable `reduce` functions, and the first that needs a per-round-creation input (which player goes first) beyond the fixed contestant list every other round type already gets.

## Design decisions

These were worked out collaboratively before any implementation, specifically because this is the most complex state machine of the three round types and getting the rules wrong would be expensive to unwind later.

- **Turn order — classic rules.** A correct consonant guess or a vowel found in the puzzle keeps the same player's turn (they choose again: spin, buy a vowel, or solve). A wrong consonant guess, a vowel not found, a `bankrupt` wedge, or a `lose-turn` wedge passes the turn to the next player in order.
- **Initial active player — `roundNumber % contestantIds.length`.** Feasible without any new persistent counter: `room-logic.ts`'s `activeRoundStateFor` already has the full `RoomState` when a round becomes active, and every queue entry already carries a `status` including `'completed'`. `roundNumber` is computed as `state.queue.filter(e => e.status === 'completed').length` and passed into `RoundModuleContext` (widened to add `roundNumber: number`, alongside the existing `roundId`/`contestantIds`) purely at round-creation time — no change to `RoundActionContext` (the per-action dispatch context) is needed.
- **Spin outcome — injected via context, not rolled inside `reduce`.** `game-room.ts` (the actual Durable Object, where side effects are already allowed) picks a real random wedge from `data.wedges` when it receives a `spin` action, then passes that pre-picked wedge into `reduceWheel` via the action context (e.g. `WheelActionContext.spinResult: WheelWedge`). The `reduce` function itself stays pure — `spin` just copies `context.spinResult` into `currentWedge` — so tests can assert every wedge outcome (bankrupt, lose-turn, cash) deterministically without touching real randomness. This mirrors how `RoundActionContext.players` is already built by the room and hand-fed to every round module.
- **Buying a vowel — charge always, pass turn on miss, funds-gated.** `buy-vowel` always deducts `vowelCost` from the buyer's `roundScores` entry, regardless of whether the letter appears, and is rejected outright if their current `roundScores` balance is below `vowelCost` — a player can't go into debt buying a vowel. If the vowel appears in the solution, the turn continues (free choice again). If it doesn't, the turn passes to the next player. No wedge is involved in a vowel buy.
- **Bankruptcy scopes to the round, never to persisted score.** `WheelState.roundScores` is a scratch bucket, separate from each `Player.score` carried over from prior rounds — this was already the existing schema stub's design, not a new decision, but is worth stating explicitly here since it's the crux of how bankruptcy behaves: a `bankrupt` wedge zeroes only the current player's `roundScores` entry, never their persisted total. Nothing a player earned in a previous round is ever at risk from a bad spin in this one. `roundScores` only becomes real, persisted score at round end (whether by a correct solve or a host `end-round`), at which point every player's `roundScores` commits to their running `Player.score` total, and the solver additionally gets `solveBonus` on top.
- **Free choice, with two gating exceptions.** At the start of a turn, and again after a turn-continuing outcome (correct consonant, vowel found), the active player has three options: `spin`, `buy-vowel`, or `attempt-solve`. Two things narrow that:
  - `spin` is rejected once every consonant in the solution has already been guessed (nothing left to land a cash wedge on); `buy-vowel` is rejected once every vowel in the solution has already been guessed/bought — in both cases the only remaining actions are the other legal one(s) and `attempt-solve`.
  - Once a player commits to `spin` or `buy-vowel`, that action must resolve before any other choice becomes available again — e.g. after `spin` lands on a cash wedge, the only legal next action is `guess-consonant` (not another spin), and landing on `bankrupt`/`lose-turn` ends the turn immediately with no further choice at all.
- **Solving — active player only, at any point in their turn, host-judged.** `attempt-solve` is legal for the active player at any time during their own turn (it doesn't require having just spun). Submitting one pauses the round in an "awaiting judgment" state — mirroring Final Jeopardy's answer-then-reveal step — during which no other action is legal until the host judges it correct or incorrect via a new host-only action. An incorrect judgment simply passes the turn (no score penalty beyond that). A correct judgment commits every player's `roundScores` to their running total, adds `solveBonus` to the solver's total, sets `solvedByPlayerId`, and completes the round.
- **No solve-guess text is ever surfaced to anyone.** A solve attempt may be spoken aloud, or entered through an entirely separate device/program, not necessarily typed into this app — so there's no guarantee any guess text the `attempt-solve` action carries reflects what the player actually said. `pendingSolve` therefore stores only `{ playerId: string }`, never a guess string, and the host judges based on what actually happened (in the room or over whatever channel the guess came through), not on comparing text to the solution. This also settles a question that would otherwise need its own answer: other contestants only ever see that *a* solve attempt is pending and for whom — there's no guess text to withhold or show in the first place.
- **Cash wedges pay by occurrence.** Landing on a cash wedge and guessing a consonant that appears in the solution pays `wedge.value * occurrences`, not a flat `wedge.value` — a `$500` wedge on a letter appearing three times pays `$1500`. This matches the classic rule and rewards guessing common letters.
- **Stuck states — host manual override only, no auto-skip.** Unlike Final Jeopardy's automatic no-show skip, Wheel of Fortune relies entirely on host judgment calls (every guess/vowel/solve resolution already routes through host-visible state), so a disconnected active player or a puzzle nobody is solving is handled by two new host-only actions rather than automatic detection: one to force-advance the turn, and one to end the round outright (revealing the full solution; accumulated `roundScores` still commit to totals as normal, just with no `solvedByPlayerId`/solve bonus).
- **Action authorization.** `spin`, `guess-consonant`, `buy-vowel`, and `attempt-solve` are only legal for the current `activePlayerId`, never any other contestant. `judge-solve`, `skip-turn`, and `end-round` are host-only, same as every other host-arbitration action in the other round types. `reduce` rejects any action from the wrong requester with an error, mirroring the host/contestant gating already explicit in the Final Jeopardy milestone.
- **Guess validity.** `guess-consonant` rejects a vowel, `buy-vowel` rejects a consonant, and either rejects a letter already present in `guessedLetters` — a repeat guess (whether it hit or missed the first time) is always an error, never a silent no-op or a second charge/turn-pass.

## What

- **`schema` amendments** (extending, not replacing, the existing `wheel-of-fortune.ts` contract):
  - Add host-only actions to `wheelActionSchema`: `judge-solve` (`{ type: 'judge-solve', correct: boolean }`), `skip-turn` (force-advance without a spin/guess), and `end-round` (host-forced completion without a solve).
  - Add a `pendingSolve: { playerId: string } | null` field to `WheelState` so the "awaiting judgment" pause is explicit state, not inferred — deliberately no guess text, per the decision above.
  - Add a fixed `contestantIds: string[]` field to `WheelState` (set once at `createInitialState`, mirroring `FinalJeopardyState`), so turn rotation (`activePlayerId` advancing to "the next player in order") has a stable, ordered roster to rotate through instead of re-deriving order from `RoundActionContext.players` on every action.
  - Add whatever minimal field marks the round complete when ended by the host rather than solved (`solvedByPlayerId` alone can't distinguish "still playing" from "host ended it with nobody correct").
  - Add `canSpin: boolean` and `canBuyVowel: boolean` to `WheelContestantView`, computed server-side against the real solution (matchable consonants/vowels not yet guessed) — a contestant only ever sees the masked `revealedSolution`, so they structurally can't determine "any vowels left to buy" themselves (e.g. a puzzle with no `U` has only 4 matchable vowels, not 5; guessing every *attempted* vowel isn't the same as exhausting every vowel *the puzzle actually contains*). The host view can compute the equivalent directly from the unmasked `WheelState` it already receives, so no schema addition is needed there.
- **`party`**: a `wheel-of-fortune` round module following the Jeopardy/Final Jeopardy modules' shape — `createInitialState` (using the new `roundNumber` to pick the first active player), `reduce` (dispatching all seven action types, enforcing every rule above), `isComplete`, `toContestantView` (masking unguessed letters in `revealedSolution`, computing `canSpin`/`canBuyVowel` from the real solution, everything else passed through as-is per the existing `WheelContestantView` shape), plus `resolveMedia`/`listMediaUrls` if puzzles ever carry media (not required for v1 — text-only puzzles are enough to start).
- **`RoundModuleContext`** widened to add `roundNumber: number`, computed once in `room-logic.ts`'s `activeRoundStateFor` from completed-queue-entry count.
- **`WheelActionContext`** (module-local, mirroring `FinalJeopardyActionContext`) carries `spinResult: WheelWedge` for the room to inject on a `spin` action.
- **`player-app`**: a `WheelBoard` — host view (puzzle board with masked/revealed letters, wedge/turn indicator, judge controls for a pending solve attempt, skip-turn/end-round overrides) and contestant view (their own spin/buy-vowel/solve controls when it's their turn, read-only board otherwise). The "Buy a Vowel" control is disabled client-side when either gating condition is already true — insufficient funds (`roundScores[selfId] < vowelCost`) or `canBuyVowel === false` from `WheelContestantView`; likewise "Spin" is disabled when `canSpin === false`. Neither check requires the client to see the actual solution — both booleans arrive pre-computed. A hand-authored fixture puzzle and a lobby "Add Wheel fixture round" button, matching the pattern from the other two round types.
- Unit tests covering: initial active player from `roundNumber % contestantIds.length`; turn continues on a correct guess/found vowel and passes on a wrong guess/miss/bankrupt/lose-turn; a cash wedge pays `value * occurrences`, not a flat value; `spin`/`buy-vowel` rejected once every matchable consonant/vowel *actually in the solution* has been guessed (not merely every letter attempted — e.g. a puzzle with no `U` disables buying vowels once A/E/I/O are gone, even though `U` itself was never tried); a repeat letter guess, a vowel submitted via `guess-consonant`, and a consonant submitted via `buy-vowel` are all rejected; any action from a non-active contestant (or a host-only action from a non-host) is rejected; a solve attempt blocks all other actions until `judge-solve`, and `pendingSolve` never carries guess text; a correct judgment commits scores and completes the round; an incorrect one just passes the turn; `skip-turn`/`end-round` work as host overrides; contestant view masks unguessed letters correctly (including multi-word rows with spaces) and reports `canSpin`/`canBuyVowel` accurately.

**Explicitly out of scope**: real round import via `builder-app` (fixture only, as with the other two round types); timers on turns; visual polish beyond functional; media (images/audio) in Wheel puzzles.

## Why

Wheel of Fortune is deliberately the last of the three v1 round types because it's the hardest: it needs real randomness threaded through an otherwise-pure architecture, a turn-based economy with several mutually exclusive legal-action states, and (per this milestone's decisions) host-mediated judgment for solves rather than automatic string matching — which is a different shape than either prior round type used. Working out these rules on paper first, rather than discovering them mid-implementation, is specifically to avoid re-deriving the phase model (à la Final Jeopardy's `revealStage`) after code already exists around a wrong assumption.

## Implementation deltas

A few things changed from the plan above once the code and a real three-tab playtest existed:

- **`spinResult` injection moved out of `game-room.ts`.** The design above has the room picking the random wedge directly; in practice that put wheel-specific logic in the shared `handleRoundAction` dispatcher. It was replaced with an optional `RoundModule.prepareActionContext?(state, data, action)` hook — called generically for every round type before `reduce` — that the wheel module alone implements to roll the wedge and merge `spinResult` into the action context. `game-room.ts` no longer imports or references any specific round type by name; `RoundActionContext` carries an untyped `[key: string]: unknown` bag for whatever a module's `prepareActionContext` injects.
- **`WheelState`/`WheelContestantView` gained `lastSpinResult: WheelWedge | null`.** Not in the original schema list — added after playtesting showed no feedback appeared when a spin landed on `bankrupt`/`lose-turn` (a cash wedge was visible via `currentWedge`, but non-cash outcomes cleared it immediately with nothing to show). `spin` now sets it on every outcome; it persists across turns until the next spin so the UI always has something to display.
- **`attempt-solve` dropped its `guess` field.** The original schema carried a `guess: string` the reducer never read (the design intentionally never trusts guess text — see "No solve-guess text is ever surfaced to anyone" above). Playtesting surfaced that the UI nonetheless required typing something into a text input to submit, which contradicted the host-judged-by-ear design. The field was removed from `wheelActionSchema` entirely; contestants now submit a bare `attempt-solve` via a plain button.
- **Buy-a-vowel is a two-step flow, not five always-visible buttons.** The original "Buy a Vowel" UI showed all five vowel letters immediately, disabled per-letter. Playtesting found this inconsistent with `spin` (a single distinct action). It's now: a "Buy a Vowel" button alongside "Spin"/"Solve" at turn start, which reveals a vowel letter picker (with a Cancel) on click — mirroring the consonant-guess picker shown after a cash spin.
- **Host and contestant "End round"/judge controls disable once the round is complete.** A repeat-click bug let `end-round` (and, by the same gap, `judge-solve`/`skip-turn`) re-apply `roundScores` on top of already-committed totals, since nothing checked whether the round had already concluded. Fixed with a reducer-level guard (`isWheelComplete(state)` rejects all three actions once `solvedByPlayerId` or `endedByHost` is set) plus a matching client-side `disabled` on the host board's buttons.
